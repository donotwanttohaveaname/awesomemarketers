Finnish marketing salary report 2026

What Finnish marketers really earn in 2026

By Anna Pogrebniak, Founder Published 23 July 2026 · Last updated 23 July 2026 13 min read
TL;DR

The median marketing salary in Finland in 2026 is €4,658 per month gross, based on 148 marketers surveyed by The Awesome Marketers. The middle 50% earn €3,738–€5,667. Nominal pay is up 16.4% since 2022, but inflation ran 19% over the same period, so real pay is down 2.6%.

Key takeaways

  • The median marketing salary in Finland is €4,658 per month gross; half of 148 respondents earn less, half earn more.
  • The career ladder still pays: medians climb from €3,333 at junior level to €7,500 at director level, and the biggest jump (+41%) sits behind the narrowest door.
  • Marketers investing in AI skills earn a median €550 per month more than those who are not, roughly €6,600 a year.
  • 56% of marketers want fully or primarily remote work, but only 17% actually get to work fully remote.
  • 74% were touched by a layoff, budget cut, downsizing, or restructure in the past 12 months, and 61% are compromising their career plans.

01📊

The headline numbers: median, spread, and the inflation gap

The median monthly gross salary for marketers in Finland is €4,658 in 2026, taken straight from person #75 of the 148 respondents with usable salary data. The mean is slightly higher at €4,787. The lowest reported salary is €1,200 per month, the highest €10,000. The 25th percentile sits at €3,738, the 75th at €5,667, and the 90th at €6,500. In practice: if you earn inside the €3,738–€5,667 band, you are normal. Below €3,738, you are probably underpaid. Above €5,667, you are in the top quarter.

Four headlines define this year's data. First, the middle: €4,658, half earn less, half earn more. Second, the inflation gap: the nominal median is up 16.4% since 2022, but inflation ran 19% over the same stretch, which leaves real pay down 2.6%. Finnish marketers earn roughly the same in real terms as they did in 2022.

Third, the year of layoffs: 74% of respondents were hit by at least one of a company layoff (46%), a marketing budget cut (31%), a team downsize (24%), or a role restructure (25%) in the past 12 months. Only 26% were untouched. Fourth, the career detour: 61% are compromising their career plans in some way, which we unpack in the mood section.

What each marketing role earns in Finland

Marketing roles in Finland span a €2,084 range between the best-paid and worst-paid role families. We grouped 138 free-text job titles into 14 role families and hid any group with fewer than 3 people. Head and Director roles top the table at €5,917. Content Marketing sits at the bottom at €3,833. The two biggest groups, Marketing Manager (n=28) and Specialist / Coordinator (n=27), bracket the overall median from both sides.

The Finnish marketing career ladder, rung by rung

The Finnish marketing career ladder still pays real money, roughly €3.3k to €5.7k across the arc, but the rungs are unevenly spaced. Here is the median at each level, with the jump from the rung below.

Three patterns stand out. Mid to Senior is the easy rung: +€800 for doing broadly the same job with more autonomy. Senior to Lead is where individual contributors stall (+6%, the trap floor) while managers pull ahead. Lead to Director is the biggest jump (+41%) through the narrowest door: only about 9% of the survey made it. And founders? They earn 44% less than the directors they often used to be.

Management itself is the dividing line. Marketers without direct reports (n=114) have a median of €4,438, a P75 of €5,300, and a P90 of €5,900. That €5,900 is effectively the ceiling for individual contributors.

Marketers with direct reports (n=34) have a median of €5,688, a P75 of €6,667, and a P90 of €8,625. Just starting out instead of climbing? Our first marketing job guide covers the bottom of this ladder.

04🎛️

Six levers that actually move marketing pay

Six levers move marketing pay in Finland more than anything else, ranked here by euros per month:

  1. Main expertise: moving from Social Media to Marketing Strategy = +€2,432/month.
  2. Team size: going from a team of just you to a team of 9+ people = +€1,733/month.
  3. Company type: moving from non-profit or public sector to enterprise or corporate = +€1,391/month.
  4. Becoming a manager: +€1,250/month, the biggest single-decision lever on the list.
  5. Learning leadership skills: +€792/month.
  6. Learning AI: +€550/month.

Notice what is not on the list: working harder at the same desk. Every lever is a change of scope, employer, or skill portfolio, not a performance review. The first three require changing what you do or where you do it. The last three you can start this quarter.

05🤖

AI skills are now a pay signal

Marketers who invest in AI skills earn a median of €4,750 per month (n=105). Marketers who do not earn €4,200 (n=43). That €550 monthly gap is roughly €6,600 a year, and it exists today, not in some projected future. 71% of marketers with a salary on file are already investing in AI skills; the 29% who are not earn less right now. Expect the gap to be small this year and bigger next year.

Does speaking Finnish increase your salary?

Speaking Finnish barely moves marketing pay in Finland. We looked at 124 usable responses from marketers at Finnish-headquartered companies and split them by working language and Finnish fluency:

Company language × your FinnishMedian monthly grossRespondents

Finnish workplace, fluent Finnish€4,65820
Finnish workplace, no fluent Finnishhidden (too few)2

The biggest group by far, 75 people, works in English without fluent Finnish. That is the Finnish marketing default in 2026. Adding fluent Finnish inside an English-speaking company is worth about €200 per month. Moving from that default to a Finnish-speaking company adds only €58. The overall correlation between Finnish fluency and pay is weak, +0.168. The takeaway is that language is not the story; company type is. Pick an English-first company, then treat Finnish as a tiebreaker, not a lever. It fits the wider picture: 82% of respondents work in English day-to-day, and four in five work at a company whose operating language is English.

07🏝️

Remote vs office: the gap marketers actually feel

Remote work is where marketers' preferences and employers' policies diverge most. Of the 151 respondents who answered both questions, 56% (84 people) want to work fully or primarily remote. Only 17% (25 people) actually get to work fully remote. The full preference breakdown: fully remote 13% (20), primarily remote 42% (64), 50/50 hybrid 31% (47), primarily in-office 11% (16), fully in-office 3% (4).

The friction point: 25% of respondents (37 people) want fully or primarily remote work but their employer pulls them in at least one day a week. That is one in four marketers commuting against their stated preference. Remote has become the new non-negotiable in how marketers talk about their next role, while most employers still expect hybrid or office presence. 08🌡️

The mood: pay is up, confidence is not

The mood, not the pay, is the real headline of 2026. Start with what happened around people: in the past 12 months, 46% saw company layoffs, 31% saw the marketing budget cut, 24% saw their team downsized, 25% had their role restructured, 12% were personally laid off, and 4% went through a temporary layoff (lomautus). Overall, 108 people out of 147, or 74%, reported at least one of these events. Only 26% got through the year untouched.

Then what happened to pay: 52% got a raise in the last 12 months, 39% stayed flat, 5% took a pay cut, and 5% were not working a year ago (base 153). And yet, on job hunting (base 171): 30% are actively looking, 33% are open to opportunities, 29% are happy, and 8% are staying only because the market feels risky. That is 63% open to moving or actively looking.

On job security (base 172): 38% feel somewhat secure but more cautious, 23% very secure, 19% uncertain, 12% between jobs, and 8% insecure or worried. In total, 27% are uncertain or worried.

The contradiction in one sentence: 52% got a pay rise, yet 63% still think about leaving and 74% saw a layoff, cut, or restructure. Even among the raise-getters, 52% (41 of 79) are open to a new job. The sharpest version of this is what we consider the single most important finding of the whole survey: 61% of marketers on teams of 9+ are job hunting right now (22 of 36 looking, 14 staying). The best-paid, best-resourced marketers are the most likely to leave.

The career cost is already visible. Asked about compromises (base 168, single-select): 24% (41 people) have considered leaving Finland, 23% (38) are staying in a job they would otherwise leave, 14% (23) accepted a role below their level or pay, and only 39% (66) say their plans are unchanged. Three in five marketers are compromising their career plans in some way.

2022 vs 2026: pay rose, but the shape changed

Between 2022 and 2026, the overall median marketing salary in Finland rose from €4,000 to €4,658, a nominal gain of 16.4%. Inflation over the same period was 19%, so the real change is −2.6%. But the average hides who actually gained. The shape of the pay distribution changed more than its level:

How this compares to MMA's palkkatutkimus 2025

MMA's palkkatutkimus 2025 is the authoritative reference for Finnish marketing pay, and its numbers run lower than ours. Fielded by Aula Research Oy between 7 and 26 January 2026 (n=2,033 total, of which 521 are marketing respondents, sales excluded, monthly = annual / 12.5), it puts the marketing median at €4,340 per month, or €4,268 base salary without bonus. That median grew +6.4% year on year, from €4,080 in 2024 and €4,000 in 2023.

More MMA detail worth knowing: the women's euro in marketing is now 0.97€, up from 0.93€. By industry, industrial companies pay €4,808, services €4,240, and retail €4,377. On pay composition, 55% earn base salary only and 43% base plus bonus. And on transparency, 79% say role-level pay information is not available at their employer; in marketing specifically, only 12% have visibility.

11🔬

Methodology: how the survey was run

The survey behind this report collected responses through a public form distributed via the Awesome Marketers newsletter, LinkedIn, and Finnish marketing communities. It has been open since Q1 2026 and is still collecting; this report reflects responses through early July 2026. The interactive version was published on 18 April 2026 and updated on 3 July 2026 with fresh data. No incentives were offered, and responses were limited to one per email.

The pay analysis covers 148 marketers with usable salary data. The base cohort is respondents with a salary who are not freelancers (freelancers price differently; if that is you, use the freelance rate calculator instead).

82% work in English day-to-day and 40% work at tech startups or scale-ups. Cleaning steps: annual gross converted to monthly (annual / 12), currency symbols stripped, obvious typos fixed. Respondents without a usable salary were kept in the mood and sentiment questions but excluded from pay, which is why sentiment bases (147 to 172) differ from the pay base of 148. We report medians throughout because they resist outliers on small samples, round group medians to whole euros, and hide any group under n=3. Job titles were grouped from 138 free-text answers into 14 role families.

What we did not do: no weighting to Finnish marketing demographics, no employer or title verification, no cross-year linking of individuals. The sample skews mid-level and senior individual contributor; juniors are underrepresented. Treat this as a community snapshot, not a census.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average marketing salary in Finland in 2026?

The median monthly gross salary is €4,658, based on 148 marketers surveyed by The Awesome Marketers community. The mean is €4,787, and the middle 50% earn between €3,738 and €5,667 per month. MMA's palkkatutkimus 2025 reports a marketing median of €4,340 per month across 521 respondents.

Do marketers who use AI earn more?

Yes. Marketers investing in AI skills earn a median of €4,750 per month, compared with €4,200 for those who are not, a gap of €550 per month or roughly €6,600 per year. 71% of respondents with a salary on file already invest in AI skills.

Does speaking Finnish increase marketing pay in Finland?

Only slightly. Inside companies where the working language is English, fluent Finnish speakers earn a median €4,800 versus €4,600 for non-speakers, about €200 per month. The overall correlation between Finnish fluency and pay is weak (+0.168), and company type matters far more than language.

What is the highest-paying marketing role in Finland?

Head and Director roles pay the highest median at €5,917 per month, followed by Founder and C-level at €5,729 and Senior Marketing Manager at €5,667. By seniority, the Chief, Director and VP tier has a median of €7,500 per month. Content Marketing has the lowest role median at €3,833.

How does this survey compare to MMA's official figures?

MMA's palkkatutkimus 2025 puts the Finnish marketing median at €4,340 per month across 521 marketing respondents, €318 below this survey's €4,658. The gap reflects this survey's skew towards tech, English-first companies and mid-to-senior marketers. Anchor on MMA's figure, then adjust for company type and seniority using this report.

Is remote work common for marketers in Finland?

Not as common as marketers would like. 56% want to work fully or primarily remote, but only 17% actually work fully remote. One in four marketers wants remote work but is pulled into the office at least one day a week.

How many marketers in Finland are job hunting in 2026?

30% are actively looking and another 33% are open to opportunities, so 63% would consider a move. On teams of nine or more people, 61% are job hunting right now. Only 29% describe themselves as happy where they are.

Did marketing salaries in Finland keep up with inflation?

No. The nominal median rose 16.4% between 2022 and 2026, from €4,000 to €4,658, but cumulative inflation over the same period was 19%. In real terms, Finnish marketers earn 2.6% less than in 2022.
